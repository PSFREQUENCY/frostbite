# Degen Dungeon 1

The official name is Degen Dungeon 1; the game's id stays `degen-dungeon`. Wake up broke in Nakasato (Na'ka Sa'ta), an island city of nine boroughs that is New York, Los Angeles and Philadelphia folded together,
and climb from Newbie to Whale over seven story days: up to three story quests a day, and six side quests beside them. A run is one day
of one hero. DUN is in-game credit only.

## Play

- **Tap or Enter to play** on the title (or the act button on a phone). That starts the run: the cinematic rolls over the city (the
  block of ice, the island growing, the signs, the name of the place; a tap, Enter or an arrow skips it, and it plays once a visit),
  then, on a game's first day, the hero ring. Choose with the arrows or a tap on either side, and wake the hero with Enter or a tap
  in the middle; the day starts then. **The whole game is played with that hero:** later days skip the ring and wake them straight
  after the cinematic (or at once). Once the story is won the ring is offered at the start of every day. The hero goes into each day's
  record, as before.
- **Your loft.** Every hero has a loft on their borough's middle street: you wake in bed there each story day, with a sofa, a screen, a
  kitchen and the freezer. Your landlord waits on the street by the door. Rent is due by nightfall (100 DUN on day one, rising to 600);
  unpaid at nightfall, your bed, freezer and boxes go out on the kerb, and a seed paper hidden in the bed or the freezer is found and
  drains your wallet. Pay the rent and a 100 DUN late fee to get back in. Sleep with the rent unpaid and you wake on the kerb the next
  morning. Once it is paid, the landlord sells upgrades in turn, kept from day to day: a patio by the door (500), a balcony (1,500) and
  the top floor with the view of the city (5,000). Your own online shop runs from the laptop on the desk by the door and sells while
  you are out: a DUN a second on day one, a DUN more a second each day after, up to a 3,000 DUN till; its screen shows the till live.
  Short of the rent, Pip and the beacon send you to the laptop first, and in the last two minutes before nightfall the rent comes first.
- **Pick a hero** on the 3D ring: ten of them, each with five stats and a story of their own. Volt, Nib, Lumen and Hrimm are Frost Run's
  own models; Omen, Chip, Mint, Rally, Dip and Slip (hidden until the Great Airdrop) are built in code.
- **The seven days.** The FREE FROST riot morning, a first day of honest hustle, the mania, the winter (it opens on the morning of the
  drain and ends in the Great Airdrop), Launch Day, Moon Night, and the morning after. Billboards, light, snow and crowds change with
  each era (the riot morning and the hustle share one, and so do Moon Night and its morning after).
- **Quests.** Pip's line says which quest you are on ("2/3") and what it asks; the next place glows as a gold beacon, and a cheer comes
  over the picture as each quest or side quest is done. Sleeping at home once a day's quests are done opens the next day.
  - Day 1: wake in your loft and walk out into the FREE FROST riot packed in front of the Global DEX, a fortress of a stock exchange on a
    granite podium, and hear Frost's speech from its steps. Pick up the wallet the crowd left in the gutter and give it back to Pearl in
    ADOR, the department store across the street; as you step back out, a thief snatches Rosa's purse at the door. Run him down on foot
    and give Rosa her purse (a side quest): he glows red, with a red arrow over his head, and the beacon and the map follow him. Then take your first DUN at the Faucet and decide where your seed paper lives. A paper in a
    pocket or on a phone gets found, and the pocket drains live until the hideout terminal stops it.
  - Day 2: finish an honest gig, ride the Green Line, buy some of the coin of the day on the Exchange floor.
  - Days 3 to 5 follow the hero's own story, three beats a day. Some beats need the right crewmate, frost vision, DUN or a trade; some ask
    a question with two answers.
  - Day 6, Moon Night: the hero's dream, then the Degen Dash run to the finish (won or not), then the party under Moon Tower. The
    lantern after dark is a side quest now.
  - Day 7: gather two crewmates, open the Genesis Room with your crew's three words, and run the oldest tunnel to SHA Beach with Max's
    Vests behind. The beach's ending tells the dream chosen on Moon Night (the save carries it).
- **Side quests**, six a day and never needed: S's three letters on the day's avenues, a landmark of the day, and four that belong to
  the day (the riot on the first morning, the saucers in the mania, the storm in the winter, a moonshot, a rooftop and the lantern on
  Moon Night...).
  The backpack lists the day's quests and side quests with ✓ for done and → for the one you are on.
- **The city:** honest gigs, a crew of three, the Green Line Express (a glass lift up to the station at each stop, five seconds on an
  open car along the elevated rails to the next stop, the lift down; the ride counts), the skate park (grab a board off the rack and
  skate five seconds round the park for the day's skate, with an ollie every few seconds), the Exchange floor's coin, MAXX (twenty a day, until the winter),
  the fit shop, the realtor and the garage, a scooter, a car, a yacht and a helicopter, and the scams: Kiki, the famous face, the
  recovery expert, the dream job, the fake Fed, the permission slip, the sticker on the meter and the honeypot coin.
- **Heat:** stealing earns stars. Two bring a beat cop who fines you; three bring a tux who freezes what he touches; at four even the
  sky is not safe and the wallet locks. Lie low in the hideout under your home, or pay the rebrand booth. Nobody is ever hurt.
- **Frost vision** (V, or its button) shows hidden words, the watcher's footprints and the tells of a scam: fifteen seconds of it (thirty
  by Lumen's lamp), topped back up every thirty seconds.
- **The mystery:** twenty-one letters signed S lie on the avenues, three each day; three street words open a dead locker; the watcher
  leaves footprints at one corner a day. The game never says who S is.
- **Status** over your head: GM, SAFU, GRINDER, WAGMI, TOUCHING GRASS, BASED, DIAMOND HANDS, DOWN BAD, PAPER HANDS, DEGEN, BAGHOLDER,
  EXIT LIQUIDITY, RUGGED, REKT or FUDDED.
- **Living streets:** low buildings with rounded corners and little shops on the ground floor ring every block, with round and
  soft-cornered towers set back behind them; lamps, hydrants, benches, bins, newspaper boxes, traffic lights, posters, flyers and a
  billboard over every crossroads; people strolling and stopping to talk; traffic that thickens at rush hour and thins at night; a sun
  that crosses the sky, then the moon and the stars. Walk into seventeen shops (sliding doors, a counter, a clerk): coffee and noodles
  calm FOMO, food and glow sticks give frost vision back, a cut or a wash loses heat, the gym and the bait shop are good for standing,
  the pawn shop buys loot, and the arcade has four cabinets, one go each a day. Each laundromat has five washers: feed one 5 DUN at
  the counter and see what the last load left (loose change, an outfit that loses the heat, or lint), each machine once a day. The
  shops are at work: drums turn (hard through a wash), arcade screens flicker, the pizza oven glows, coffee and noodles steam.
- **Landmarks, all open:** walk into round halls with a doorway and an arched canopy, each with one thing to do once a day: dance in
  three clubs (The Mempool, Club Halving, Block Party), catch the late show at Starlight Cinema (three short original films play on
  its screen in turn), plug into a pod at The Dome VR, play
  the Mega Arcade's cabinets, read the whitepaper at Whitepaper Library, see the exhibits at the Museum of Genesis, collect a print at
  Pixel Vault Gallery, cut a track at Bassline Records, go live at Clipstream Studios, and freeze or thaw DUN at First Block Bank's
  teller. The Meme Market Skate Park and Gig Street Green are open to the sky. Lookout posts, NFT vending machines and company names on
  the tallest towers finish it. Every name is an original gag.
- **Rides on the street:** cars, scooters, bikes, minivans, motorbikes, pickups and semi cabs are parked along the kerbs. Anything with
  an engine brings a star of heat; a scooter or a bike is free. The hero sits in the saddle and pedals a bike, sits astride a motorbike,
  stands on a scooter's deck, and climbs in through a swinging driver's door to sit inside the rest, behind tinted glass. The crew take
  the other seats; whoever has no seat runs alongside. Step off anywhere (act again) and the ride stays where you left it, the last six
  of them, to be taken again.
- **The saucers:** in the mania (day 3), a quarter of the way in, flying saucers come down over four street crossings with their beams
  on the ground, and more circle the sky; townspeople float up every beam, one after another. Stand still in a beam and you float up
  too. Frost vision lights the clues: the glow sticks, the pizza, the magnet and the vacuum glow in columns of gold seen through walls,
  and pulse on the map. Glow sticks from the hardware store and a pizza
  make peace (standing, and frost vision); a bag of the Exchange's meme coin can be traded for three times what the market pays. Go up
  with nothing to offer, or go quietly, and you pass out, see the moon go by, and wake in your own bed on the first morning of the story
  again. The first peace or trade ends the alarm and that saucer flies home; the other three stay, and on every day after the mania
  they come back as friends at the same hour (they never take you with nothing to offer, and you can ask to be set down). Each peace
  or trade is a beam-up, one a saucer a day, counted in your save: at thirteen the visitors leave you a saucer of your own on a lawn
  of the Old Floe. Fly it like the helicopter, only faster (R and F, or Climb and Drop, take it up and down). The backpack keeps the
  count, and each beam-up gets a cheer.
- **Bumping into people:** walk into someone on the street (or in the riot or the party) and they stagger aside; run into them and they
  go down, sit a moment and get back up. In front of a ride most hop clear, and the rest are knocked over, roll and get up. Nobody is
  hurt, and the street's people are only drawn, so a bump never changes the day.
- **Weather:** three times a day the sky may turn harsh for three minutes: a blizzard, thick fog, a thunderstorm with lightning, a gale,
  snow or a heatwave. Fog, snow and blizzards hide you (heat fades twice as fast), a heatwave keeps it on you longer, and storms and gales
  make FOMO climb twice as fast. A player of good standing sees calmer skies.
- **Money on the move:** every DUN in or out floats up beside the readout (green in, red out) with a chime and a digital receipt: the
  amount, the new balance, the block and a transaction id.
- **The Degen Dash:** from the second day, Miss Mirinda, the Dash's host (an Afro-hawk, a turquoise two-piece and a lot of gold), sells tickets
  (250 DUN) at her arch on the avenue north of Ticker Square, and now and then walks up to a hero on foot who has none and waits beside
  them (act to talk to her). Mastering one of the ten top skills on a Dash day earns a free ticket from whoever taught it (the courier
  desk for a third gig, the park keeper for grass touched, a rooftop runner for a roof crossed, and so on; three at most). Hand a ticket
  to Miss Mirinda at her arch and the city lights the way: a ribbon of light runs along the streets from gate to gate, the leg to the
  next gate pulsing gold, and the city's people pack both sides of it like a marathon, cheering with signs held high. With a ticket,
  act at the arch to start the clock, then clear nine stages in order round the Exchange's
  borough, each its own way: sprint through the first gate (running), duck under a low bar (crouching), crawl under a net, climb the
  wall to its top storey, cross the roof to the flag, find a mark only frost vision shows, take the Dash car (it waits by the north
  street) through the checkpoint on the east street, answer Miss Mirinda's crypto-safety question at her desk (a wrong answer adds ten
  seconds), and get back to the arch. Pip's line shows the stage, what it asks and the clock; the beacon points at the next gate.
  Under par (1:40) and with no heat is a win: 50,000 DUN (once a day), the trophy and a champion's print in the backpack, and a parade
  (confetti, fireworks over the arch, the trophy held up over your head, and the street lining the avenue to cheer). Slower is a
  finish and a finisher's medal (1,000 DUN once a day). After six minutes the course closes. Finishing it is Moon Night's second quest.
- **Day two's concert:** a crew pastes LUX LIVE TONIGHT posters on The Exchange's middle street and hands out flyers, and a flyer is
  the pass at the Floe Amphitheater's stadium gate (turnstiles, guards and walk-through metal detectors) on the Old Floe's north-east
  lawn. Late in the day the manager at the stage door says the show is off without Lux's spirit625, an alcohol-free fizz from Omi Omy,
  ADOR's drinks bar. Bring one and the show goes on: thirty seconds of close-ups, the band, her dance and the crowd, with the lights
  sweeping (a side quest). After it, Miss Mirinda dances at The Long Game (the dance studio that was Block Party): three moves on their
  counts, a wrong one starts again, and three right impress her (a side quest). Lux's show teaches the moves. Day two's other side
  quests: go live, play a cabinet, three gigs.
- **Powers (Q, or the Power button):** every hero has their own, which cools for twenty seconds after each use and grows a level
  (to three) for each top skill mastered that day. The ram charges and senses S's nearest letter (a gold beam points the way); the
  dragon takes off and flies, ten seconds a level and quicker than running: low over the streets and the sea at level one, high over
  every rooftop from level two or from a roof (climb a building, or the WAGMI sign, and take off from the top), rising over the roofs
  it crosses (Pip counts the seconds; the power again lands it, and it comes down on open ground); the miner digs
  anywhere and now and then turns up DUN or a gem, which spins up out of the hole; the ice giant breathes frost that freezes whoever
  is close ahead; the wizard blinks a few metres in a puff of sparks; the dog sniffs and the cat sees with frost vision (the cat for
  longer); the bull charges in streaks of speed; the bear's stamp kicks up dust and stops the tux and the beat cop round it; and the
  unicorn's shimmer swirls a rainbow and cools a star of heat. Every power glows round its hero while it works.
- **The crystal caves and the Underground Bazaar:** a crack in the rocks in the island's south-east corner opens into caves that wind
  east and down, eight metres in all, past walls of shining quartz, diamond, amethyst, emerald and rose crystal on a glossy floor, to a
  back door of the Underground Bazaar. Come in that way without having taken the main stairs (on The Shaft's street) today, and heads
  turn all down the stalls while the guard walks you up to the main door. Through the main door, three honest traders: one buys the
  gems a miner digs up (300 DUN each), one sells crystal shards, one tells the phrase of the day.
- **The Light Re-emitter:** the Time Machine is the Light Re-emitter now. The trip back to day one still opens when a statue rises, and
  its ring of light beside the Ice Runner in The Old Floe sends your light to your loft, the Global DEX, the Floe Amphitheater, the
  crystal caves or the bazaar's stairs, any day.
- **Food and hunger:** hunger builds from your last meal to starving in five minutes, and Pip nags until you eat. Three Crypto Shacks
  sell a Satoshi Burger (15 DUN) at the counter, or through the drive-through if you pull up at the wheel (a day-five side quest); the
  first shack's waitress has lost her dog Biscuit, who is sniffing round the Floe Food Court's stalls (noodles, tacos, smoothies) on
  the Old Floe's south-west lawn: bring her home for 100 DUN and a free meal (a day-four side quest). Streaming live from the pod in The
  Dome VR pays 50 DUN every ten seconds, but only when fed and only for a minute: then the stream cuts and you are starving until you
  eat. Diamond Hands, the seafront restaurant, serves the Diamond Plate (500 DUN, a last-day side quest); its owner Ms. Opal, in
  turquoise, runs the Diamond Hands gang, who walk up to anyone who has made 50,000 DUN today with a diamond NFT "for protection"
  (5,000 DUN). Turn them down and they tip off the tuxes: a star of heat. The gas stations are Silky now, each with a sign that spins
  on a pole by day and glows neon after nightfall.
- **The lantern night:** after dark the lantern ground in The Old Floe opens. Write a message, pick a wish (clear sight, respect, a
  cold freezer or a full pocket) and send it up among thousands; the next story day, the crypto gods may answer.
- **The moonshot and the moon:** stake 1,000, 5,000 or 10,000 DUN at the Moonshot Launchpad under Moon Tower; it rides the next launch
  (every thirty seconds), where about one in thirty goes a hundred times up and the rest go to nothing. A lucky shot pays for a seat on
  the shuttle (500,000 DUN, pocket first, then the freezer, on foot and with no heat). Up there, in a helmet, walk the moon's ground,
  meet the colony, take a rock from a crater rim, and fly home from the pad.
- **Cinematic clips:** walking into a shop or landmark for the first time today, finishing an activity or a story beat, a letter, a
  ride, a lantern, a moonshot landing or rugging, and the shuttle each play a short letterboxed clip with a title. The day waits while
  it plays, and any key or tap skips it.
- **Nine statues**, one on the middle street of each borough: athletes carved at full sprint (chest, shoulders, arms, flat hands, thighs,
  knees, calves, feet on their toes), each in its own stone and its own mask: the Harbor Sprinter's gold visor, the Tide Leaper's fin,
  the Ice Runner's blank white mask, the Neon Ghost's mirror visor, the Golden Bull's horns, the Grinner's grin, the Weaver's stripes,
  the Digger's lamp and the Sunburst's rays. **They wake once the story is won** (a last day ended on SHA Beach): from then on act at
  one to put its mask on and play the day on as that statue come to life, a stride quicker on foot, and act at it again to take it
  off. Before that the note says the mask is waiting. Nobody real is carved: every figure is an original.
- **Walk and drive anywhere outdoors:** the streets and their three-metre pavements, the plazas round every landmark's hall right up to
  its curved wall, the skate park, the green and the Old Floe's three lawns (all open straight from the pavement; sitting still on the
  lawns cools FOMO like the park's middle); inside a hall the floor runs right out to its round wall.
- **Busy halls:** every landmark is full of people doing what it is for: dancers under the club lights, a packed cinema, readers and
  browsers in the library, players at the arcade's cabinets, skaters on their boards, visitors at the museum's plinths. The Global
  DEX is a trading floor behind real security (metal detector arches, turnstiles, glass barriers, guards, cameras on every wall): four
  trading posts crowned with scrolling tickers and packed with traders shouting with their arms up, a big board of prices, a ticker
  band round the hall and a bell balcony. ADOR is a two-floor mall: eighteen shops round its walls, a gallery floor with a glass
  rail, escalators up to it, and shoppers on both floors with their bags. The Underground Bazaar is crowded: rows of stalls under
  striped canopies heaped with goods, lantern strings across the hall, a keeper at every stall and shoppers in every aisle. Bump into
  anyone and they stagger out of your way.
- **Neon pickups:** whatever can be picked up, bought or borrowed glows. The dropped wallet lies in a lime beam with gold sparkles; S's
  letters, the loot and the couriers' parcels shine; the magnet and the vacuum float over their pallets in red and violet light; the
  rides on their stands are lit along their paint with a pool of light under them, and every ride parked at a kerb has its own
  underglow, faint by day and bright after dark. The Pixel Vault Gallery is vault-dark inside now, hung with twelve original pixel-art
  pieces in pulsing neon frames, and the piece of the day waits on an easel at the back, lit gold until a print is bought (then a red
  dot, as galleries mark what has sold). The Meme Market's shiny frame glows gold at its auction.
- **The Fancy Lot's hover car:** from nightfall on the second day the Fancy Lot, a glass showroom on Haus De Savage's plaza, lends its one
  hover car on the Diamond Hands' word. It floats on its own light, is quicker than any car and crosses the sea. Keep it by running at
  least one of the Diamond Hands' jobs a day: Rocco, by the seafront restaurant's door, hands out three, one at a time (a guest driven to
  Haus De Savage's doorman in the hover car, a sealed cold wallet to the vault guard on the Global DEX's steps, and an order run over the
  sea to Ms. Opal's yacht), 10,000 DUN for all three. A day without one and the lot takes the car back the next morning.
- **Haus De Savage:** a tree of a tower on its own islet in the sea south of Gig Street, over a bridge under a neon arch. Its trunk is
  braided from bark ribs with veins of gold light, every floor is a ring of garden with a branch held out to its own tree (the sky
  garden on 7, the sky spa on 14), and three glass elevators climb its south face: step into the middle one and ride it a floor at a
  time on R and F (Climb and Drop on a phone), or open its panel for the lobby or the rooftop. The concierge sells the sky penthouse on
  the twentieth floor (50,000 DUN), with its keycard to the top two floors, a pad for the hover car (the valet's lift raises it) and a
  bed you wake up in. The rooftop has a pool, the Canopy restaurant and a helicopter that lifts off high over the islet; the dragon can
  fly off it too. While you are inside, the floors above you are cut away so the camera sees in.
- **TVs:** a commercial loops on the island's screens, a rocket climbing to the moon and the slogan "Only Masters can make it to the
  moon": on the wall of your loft, in the penthouse, at every Green Line station, and on a great curved screen over ADOR's doorway with
  the slogan scrolling on a band beneath it.
- **Neon meters:** over the picture's top-left corner, every goal at a glance: today's quests as lit units, the side quests, the rent
  against the pocket (red while short, pulsing in the last two minutes), the power charging with its level, how full the hero is, the
  shop's till, the skills mastered as units, and the way to the next rank; and while they last, the flight, the board, a live stream,
  a saucer's beam and a Degen Dash's stages, on top. A phone shows the five that matter most. The backpack lights every count the same
  way: a bar under each side quest and each of the ten top skills (its own new section), and units under each count in a title.
- **Alive:** the clubs are packed (sixty on the floor, the seats full), ADOR is packed on both floors with its escalators full, every
  walk-in shop has its customers (browsing the aisles, waiting at the counter, eating at the noodle bars, coffee shops and Diamond
  Hands, at the laundromat's machines, in the barber's chair, working out at the gym), each Silky pump has a car pulling in, filling up
  with its driver at the hose and pulling out, and every borough's middle streets have skaters rolling by and shoppers with their bags.
- **People and things, not markers:** every place is someone to talk to or something to use. People who have something to say wear a
  speech bubble, and the one the story wants next a turning gold "!". S's letters lie sealed on the pavement, scraps of paper and the
  words scratched in the ice where they fell, the loot in its sack, the courier desks' parcels beside their dispatchers, and the Faucet,
  the drop boxes, the hideout freezer, the dead locker, the humming basement's door, the Rebrand booth, the magnet and the vacuum stand
  where they are, each with a small star turning over it (gold where the quest goes). The ride stands show their rides. Each story beat
  has someone waiting with what it is about: the shiny frame's beat is an auction in the Meme Market, easels of gilt frames under an
  AUCTION sign; others get an arcade cabinet, a fuse box and a spool of wire, a miner's lamp by a carved rock, a couch, a club's door,
  a snow pile and a shovel, a notice board, a camera, crates and buckets, a rowboat and a rod, a bench, a pawn window or the
  Exchange's chart. The beacon's column still shows the way from afar.
- **Knock it off the shelf:** brush past a corner store's or a station's aisle and what you touch tumbles to the floor (running, you
  clear more of it). A moment later a stock clerk comes out from the back, picks each thing up and puts it back where it was.
- **Climb any building's wall:** from the pavement beside one (Climb on a phone, R on a keyboard), a storey at a time up to three, and
  back down with Drop or F. Up a wall nobody walks, uses anything or gets on a ride; the beat cop waits underneath (he only catches feet
  on the ground), but a tux at four stars still reaches. The hero turns to the wall, climbs hand over hand and hangs there. On the
  wall, act climbs in at the window in front of you (once a window a day): a neighbour shares a snack (frost vision), an artist lets
  you sketch from the loft (standing), a flat stands empty, or somebody shuts the window on you (a little heat).
- **Walk the roofs:** from the top storey, climb once more to step onto the roof. Walk the ring of roofs round the block (not out over
  its courtyard); at an edge over a street, Drop or F climbs back down that wall.
- **Bodies that move:** every hero and every person built in code bends at the hips, knees, ankles, spine, shoulders, elbows and wrists:
  they stride and swing their arms, roll onto their toes, run leaning in with bent elbows, duck on bent knees, crawl on hands and knees,
  climb, hang and tuck their legs in a jump. Frost Run's models play their own Duck and Hop clips and bend their bones the same way to
  crawl, climb and hang; the ram's knees bend through every stride of its run, and the dragon hovers on beating wings as it flies.
- **The Time Machine:** reach a worth of 100,000 DUN (or finish Moon Night on SHA Beach) and the Ice Runner lifts off its plinth on a
  column of light. Step up to it and the next day is a trip back to day one, where your newbie self can be met three times; each answer bends the
  timeline for better or worse, and the timeline carries into the next playthrough. Come home to a present you changed (your pocket
  and your look, up or down) and you earn the Butterfly effect badge; a hero with a bent timeline wears it, golden butterflies about
  them for a brighter one and grey static for a darker one.
- **Corner stores and gas stations:** nine corner stores, each with a counter behind floor-to-ceiling bulletproof glass, shelves behind
  the clerk, aisles of snacks and a wall of lit freezers: hot chips (frost vision), a chopped cheese (standing), a juice (calmer), and ice
  pops and pints from the freezer. Six Silky stations have a forecourt with pumps and a pole sign, a hot-dog roller and a coin ATM;
  a Fuzzy (quicker on foot for half a minute), a full tank (rides with an engine a little quicker all day) and the ATM all want a
  Nakasato ID, made at the ID kiosk for 100 DUN and kept from day to day.
- **The freeze ray:** in Cold Harbor Deli's freezer, in a case of frost that only a statue's face opens. Wearing a mask, take it, and G
  (or the Freeze ray button) fires it the way you last walked: everything within twelve metres along that line and a metre either side
  freezes for thirty seconds (people, rides, the beat cop, the tux), nearest first, up to 63 at once, and the beam bends the light like
  gravity. Frozen people cannot be talked to, frozen rides cannot be taken, a frozen chaser stands still, and freezing brings heat.
  Thirty-six frozen at once earns Sub-Zero, so it takes a packed place: the riot, Moon Night's party, a Dash parade, a row of parked
  cars.
- **Mount WAGMI:** east of The Old Floe, the Floe's middle street runs on up a hiking trail (timber steps, rope on posts, a sign at the
  bottom) to a terrace on the ridge, where the WAGMI letters stand ten metres tall, about six heroes, on a scaffold of wooden planks.
  Climb any letter from the terrace in front of it (you go up at its nearest seat: a W's outer strokes or its middle, an M's stems) and
  sit on its top: the camera turns to the whole city below and the picture clears for a screenshot.
- **Badges:** every badge earned lands as a big medallion in the middle of the picture, struck in gold, silver, ice or iridescent metal
  with its own emblem, spinning with glitter and confetti, then flies up into the badge tray in the corner, which counts your
  collection.

Keys: arrows or WASD walk (up is the way the camera looks), Shift held runs, C crouches, Z crawls, Space jumps, E acts and gives the first
answer, 1 to 5 pick an answer, X walks away, B or I opens the backpack, V is frost vision, G fires the freeze ray, R and F climb a wall
or a letter, ride Haus De Savage's glass elevator a floor at a time or fly a helicopter up and down, Enter starts, P pauses. On a phone or tablet the picture fills the screen: press and hold the drawn stick's edge to walk that
way (no slide needed), or put a thumb anywhere else on the left half and slide (the ring jumps under it and the way it slides steers,
sixteen ways); a tap on the right acts, and round buttons under

## How a day is checked

`src/rules/` is a pure, seeded, whole-number state machine (`degenRules`). Settings are `{ day, hero }` (day 1 to 7), with `loop` on a
trip back, and on a story day `fate` for the timeline it carries, `boon` for last night's lantern wish, `beamups` for the beam-ups of
earlier days, `idcard` for a Nakasato ID made on an earlier day, `loft` and `owed` for the loft and its rent, `hover` for the Fancy
Lot's hover car kept from yesterday (or `repo` when it was taken back), `keycard` for the sky penthouse and, on the last day, `dream`
for the dream chosen on Moon Night.
Inputs are `move`, `gait`, `act`, `pick`, `lift`, `look`, `shoot` and `end`, each with its tick; the day runs itself between them. A jump is only on the screen. The platform
replays the record to the same end and decides the milestones from it, forty in all: twenty for the story (its quests from the seed
paper on, as Frost's speech and finishing the Dash have none, then the Degen Dash won and Moon Night's lantern), ten top skills, and
the top achievements: nine hidden ones (the Butterfly effect and Sub-Zero among them) and the saucer pilot
(`src/rules/milestones.ts`). The Dash's course and rules are `src/rules/course.ts` and `src/rules/dash.ts`; the stores, the freeze ray
and Mount WAGMI are `src/rules/stores.ts`, `src/rules/freeze.ts` (with the crowds it catches, `src/rules/crowd.ts`) and
`src/rules/mountain.ts`; Haus De Savage and its elevator are `src/rules/haus.ts`, and the Fancy Lot's deal, the Diamond Hands' jobs and
the penthouse `src/rules/deal.ts`. `src/replay.ts` publishes the rules; `src/sample-run.ts` is
a legal day the verifier is shown with. The settings come from the page, so `fate`, `boon`, `beamups` and `dream` are the page's word, as
before.

## Tests

`npx vitest run --config vitest.config.ts --coverage` from this folder. Bots (`test/support/`) play every hero's every story day to the
end, and between them earn every milestone; an idle day earns none. The Dash bot (`test/support/dash.ts`) runs the course the quick way:
about a minute (1,802 to 2,119 ticks, by the hero's speed), against a par of 3,000 ticks. The freeze bot (`test/support/freeze.ts`)
takes a mask and the ray, then sweeps the riot in front of the Global DEX with seven shots and holds more than thirty-six frozen at once.
The bots pay the landlord before they go to bed, and leave any ride at the loft's door.
